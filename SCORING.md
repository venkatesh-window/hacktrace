# Scoring Engine — Technical Documentation

The `scoring.py` module calculates **four heuristic risk scores**, each normalized to the range **[0, 1]**.

These scores act as lightweight, interpretable substitutes for more computationally intensive models such as:

* **GraphSAGE** → Mule/graph relationship analysis
* **Hawkes Process** → Transaction burst detection
* **Centrality Analysis** → ATM hub identification
* **Rossmo Geographic Profiling** → Geographic risk estimation

The four scores are then provided as features to the **XGBoost Risk Model** in `train_risk_model.py`.

---

## 1. Input Sources

The scoring engine receives its inputs primarily from two sources:

### A. Transaction Graph Data

The transaction graph is represented using a **NetworkX `DiGraph`**.

* **Nodes** → Bank accounts
* **Directed edges** → Money transfers between accounts
* **Edge attributes**:

  * `amount` → Transaction amount in INR
  * `minutes_ago` → Relative transaction timestamp
* **Node attributes**:

  * `home_ifsc` → Bank branch associated with the account

The graph is generated by:

```text
data/build_graph.py
```

and stored as:

```text
data/seed_graph.pkl
```

---

### B. Geographic and ATM Registry Data

Geographical information is obtained from:

```text
data/atms.py
```

It contains:

* `BRANCHES` → Bank branch coordinates
* `ATMS` → ATM IDs and coordinates

The account's `home_ifsc` is used to locate the mule's associated branch.

# 3. Heuristic Scoring Models

The system calculates four independent scores:

| Score         | Purpose                                    |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| `mule_score`  | Detect suspicious mule accounts            |
| `burst_score` | Detect clustered transaction activity      |
| `hub_score`   | Identify frequently reused ATMs            |
| `geo_score`   | Estimate geographic likelihood of cash-out |

All scores fall within:

$$
0 \leq Score \leq 1
$$

A higher score indicates greater risk or likelihood.

---

# 4. Mule Score

## `mule_score(G, account)`

### Purpose

The **Mule Score** identifies accounts exhibiting behavior commonly associated with mule accounts.

A suspicious mule account typically:

1. Receives money from multiple accounts.
2. Holds the money for a short period.
3. Quickly transfers the money elsewhere.
4. Passes through similar amounts.

---

## Inputs

```text
G
```

NetworkX directed transaction graph.

```text
account
```

Account being evaluated.

### Edge attributes used

```text
minutes_ago
amount
```

---

## Calculation

### Step 1 — Fan Ratio

The system compares the number of incoming and outgoing transactions:

$$
fan\_ratio =
\frac{fan\_in}
{\max(fan\_out,1)}
$$

The fan-in component is normalized:

$$
fan\_component =
\min\left(
\frac{fan\_ratio}{5.0},
1.0
\right)
$$

A higher value means the account receives money from many sources relative to its outgoing activity.

---

### Step 2 — Recency Gating

Recent activity receives greater importance.

$$
recency =
e^{-\frac{latest\_in\_time}{240}}
$$

The fan-in score is then adjusted:

$$
gated\_fan =
fan\_component
\times
(0.3 + 0.7 \times recency)
$$

This prevents old accounts, such as historical payroll accounts, from receiving an unnecessarily high mule score simply because they have high historical fan-in.

---

### Step 3 — Holding Time

The system estimates how quickly money moves through the account.

$$
holding\_minutes =
\max(
latest\_in\_time - latest\_out\_time,
0
)
$$

Then:

$$
holding\_component =
e^{-\frac{holding\_minutes}{240}}
$$

If the incoming money has **not yet moved out**, the recency value is used instead.

A shorter holding period results in a higher score.

---

### Step 4 — Pass-Through Ratio

Incoming and outgoing transaction amounts are compared.

$$
pass\_through =
1 -
\min\left(
\frac{
|avg\_in-avg\_out|
}{
\max(avg\_in,1)
},
1
\right)
$$

If incoming and outgoing amounts are similar, the score approaches **1**.

---

### Step 5 — Final Mule Score

The components are combined using weighted scoring:

$$
\boxed{
mule\_score =
0.45(gated\_fan)
+
0.35(holding\_component)
+
0.20(pass\_through)
}
$$

### Weight Distribution

| Component        |  Weight |
| ---------------- | ------: |
| Fan-in / Fan-out | **45%** |
| Holding Time     | **35%** |
| Pass-through     | **20%** |

---

# 5. Burst Score

## `burst_score(G, account)`

### Purpose

The Burst Score acts as a lightweight substitute for a **Hawkes self-exciting process**.

It detects whether transactions occur in a **tight temporal cluster** rather than being distributed over a long period.

---

## Inputs

```text
G
```

NetworkX transaction graph.

```text
account
```

Account being evaluated.

---

## Calculation

### Step 1 — Collect Transaction Times

All incoming and outgoing transaction timestamps are collected:

```text
minutes_ago
```

The timestamps are then sorted.

---

### Step 2 — Calculate Average Transaction Gap

For `N` transaction events:

$$
avg\_gap =
\frac{1}{N-1}
\sum_i
(times_{i+1}-times_i)
$$

A smaller average gap indicates more tightly clustered activity.

---

### Step 3 — Calculate Recency

$$
recency =
e^{-\frac{\min(times)}{300}}
$$

Recent activity produces a higher value.

---

### Step 4 — Calculate Burstiness

$$
burstiness =
e^{-\frac{avg\_gap}{60}}
$$

Smaller transaction gaps result in higher burstiness.

---

### Step 5 — Final Burst Score

$$
\boxed{
burst\_score =
0.5(recency)
+
0.5(burstiness)
}
$$

| Component  |  Weight |
| ---------- | ------: |
| Recency    | **50%** |
| Burstiness | **50%** |

---

# 6. Hub Score

## `hub_score(atm_id, cashout_history)`

### Purpose

The Hub Score identifies ATMs that are repeatedly used by suspicious transactions or fraud rings.

It represents **recency-weighted ATM reuse**.

---

## Inputs

```text
atm_id
```

ATM currently being evaluated.

```text
cashout_history
```

Historical cash-out events.

Example:

```json
[
  {
    "atm_id": "ATM001",
    "minutes_ago": 120
  },
  {
    "atm_id": "ATM001",
    "minutes_ago": 500
  }
]
```

---

## Calculation

### Step 1 — Filter ATM History

Only cash-outs associated with the target ATM are considered.

---

### Step 2 — Apply Recency Weight

Each historical cash-out is assigned a recency weight:

$$
weighted =
\sum_h
e^{-\frac{h["minutes\_ago"]}{10080}}
$$

The decay constant is:

```text
10,080 minutes ≈ 1 week
```

Therefore, recent ATM usage contributes more strongly than older usage.

---

### Step 3 — Normalize

The result is normalized around approximately three recent cash-outs:

$$
\boxed{
hub\_score =
\min
\left(
\frac{weighted}{3.0},
1.0
\right)
}
$$

### Interpretation

| Hub Score | Interpretation                 |
| --------: | ------------------------------ |
|       0.0 | No meaningful historical reuse |
|      ~0.5 | Moderate reuse                 |
|      ~1.0 | Strong/repeated ATM reuse      |

---

# 7. Geographic Score

## `geo_score(mule_lat, mule_lon, atm_lat, atm_lon)`

### Purpose

The Geographic Score estimates how likely a candidate ATM is to be used for cash-out based on its distance from the mule's associated branch.

It uses a simplified **Rossmo-style geographic profiling model** with:

* Distance calculation
* Buffer zone
* Exponential distance decay

---

## Inputs

### Mule / Branch

```text
mule_lat
mule_lon
```

### Candidate ATM

```text
atm_lat
atm_lon
```

---

## Step 1 — Calculate Distance

The system uses the **Haversine formula** to calculate great-circle distance.

$$
d =
2R
\arcsin
\left(
\sqrt{
\sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta\phi}{2}\right)
+
\cos(\phi_1)
\cos(\phi_2)
\sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta\lambda}{2}\right)
}
\right)
$$

where:

* \(d\) = distance in km
* \(R\) = Earth's radius
* \(\phi\) = latitude
* \(\lambda\) = longitude

---

## Step 2 — Buffer Zone

The model defines a:

```text
Buffer = 1.5 km
```

inside which the score is intentionally reduced.

If:

$$
d < 1.5
$$

then:

$$
\boxed{
geo\_score =
0.3
\times
\frac{d}{1.5}
}
$$

This models the assumption that a mule is less likely to cash out immediately adjacent to their registered home branch.

---

## Step 3 — Distance Decay

For distances outside the buffer:

$$
d \geq 1.5
$$

the score decreases exponentially.

The decay distance is:

```text
8 km
```

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
geo\_score =
e^{-\frac{d-1.5}{8}}
}
$$

### Interpretation

Closer ATMs generally receive higher geographic scores, while the score gradually decreases as distance increases.

# 9. Feature Flow into XGBoost

The four heuristic scores become input features for the downstream risk model:

$$
\boxed{
[
mule\_score,\;
burst\_score,\;
hub\_score,\;
geo\_score
]
\rightarrow
XGBoost
}
$$

The XGBoost model combines these signals to produce the final:

### **ATM Risk Probability**

and subsequently:

### **Ranked List of Likely Cash-Out Locations**

---

# 10. Overall Interpretation

| Score           | What it answers                                                         |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Mule Score**  | *Does this account behave like a mule?*                                 |
| **Burst Score** | *Are transactions happening unusually close together?*                  |
| **Hub Score**   | *Is this ATM repeatedly associated with suspicious cash-outs?*          |
| **Geo Score**   | *How geographically plausible is this ATM for the mule?*                |
| **XGBoost**     | *What is the overall risk that this ATM is a likely cash-out location?* |

### Final Concept

```text
Transaction Behavior
        +
Transaction Timing
        +
ATM Historical Usage
        +
Geographic Proximity
        ↓
Four Interpretable Risk Scores
        ↓
      XGBoost
        ↓
ATM Risk Probability
        ↓
Ranked Cash-Out Locations
```

This architecture keeps the initial scoring layer **fast, interpretable, and easy to demonstrate**, while allowing the XGBoost layer to learn how the four signals should be combined for the final prediction.
